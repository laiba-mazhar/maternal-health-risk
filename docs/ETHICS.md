# Safety, ethics, and what this tool is not

## What it is not

**Not a medical device.** Not registered, not certified, not validated on any
patient population. It has no regulatory status in Pakistan or anywhere else.

**Not a diagnostic system.** It produces a *referral suggestion* from six
vitals. It cannot detect pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, sepsis, or
anything else, and the message templates are lint-checked to make sure they
never claim to.

**Not a substitute for clinical judgement.** A Lady Health Worker who thinks a
mother needs to be seen should send her, whatever the screen says. The interface
shows the drivers behind every band precisely so that it can be argued with.

**Not evaluated on Pakistani data.** The training dataset was collected in a
South Asian community-clinic setting, but not in Pakistan. That gap is the
subject of the calibration study, not something the study resolves.

## The failure that matters

The dominant risk is a **critical miss**: a genuinely high-risk mother
classified low risk and sent home. Every design decision in the project is
downstream of that:

* the cost matrix prices a critical miss at 25× an unnecessary referral;
* model selection is on high-risk recall, not accuracy, and does pick a
  less accurate model as a result;
* the decision rule escalates on ordered probability thresholds rather than
  `argmax`, so a 40% chance of high risk escalates even when low risk holds the
  plurality;
* `critical_miss_rate` is reported separately from recall, because predicting
  "mid risk" for a high-risk mother still sends her onward, while "low risk"
  does not.

The second risk is quieter and is what actually kills screening tools:
**over-referral**. A tool that flags 80% of mothers creates work nobody can
absorb, gets ignored within a month, and then provides no protection at all.
That is why the referral rate is a first-class metric, why threshold tuning runs
under an explicit referral budget (`config.MAX_REFERRAL_RATE`), and why the
headline comparison against the guideline baseline is made at *matched referral
load* rather than at each system's own convenient operating point.

## Message design

Three commitments, enforced by `localization.lint_templates` and tested in CI
rather than left to reviewer diligence:

1. **No catastrophising.** Urgency lives in the action and its timeframe
   ("today", "within a few days"), never in frightening adjectives. A family
   that panics may go nowhere, or to the wrong place. Terms like خطرناک and
   "life-threatening" are banned outright.
2. **No diagnosis.** The tool reports observations and who to see. Naming a
   condition it cannot confirm spreads misinformation faster than a correct
   referral travels.
3. **Family-inclusive framing at the high band.** Where care decisions are made
   collectively, a message addressed only to the patient can stall against a
   household that was never consulted. High-risk messages name the family as
   part of the plan — not to bypass the mother's consent, but because a referral
   she cannot act on alone is not a referral.

A fourth, added after reviewing the rendered output: **the low band mentions no
drivers at all.** Telling a mother her result is normal and then listing which
of her vitals "stood out" plants worry the result does not justify, and worry is
what makes a family ignore the next message.

## Review status: UNREVIEWED

The Urdu templates are hand-authored and lint-clean. They have **not** been:

* reviewed by a native-speaker reviewer for register and regional idiom;
* reviewed by a clinician for clinical appropriateness;
* tested with any Lady Health Worker or patient.

`localization.review_summary()` reports `UNREVIEWED` for all three bands, the
Streamlit app renders that status as a visible warning on every result, and the
status is written into `run_metadata.json`. "Plausible Urdu written by the
project author" is not "signed off", and the artifact says so rather than
letting a reader assume otherwise.

Promoting a template requires setting `Template.review` to
`LANGUAGE_REVIEWED` or `CLINICALLY_REVIEWED` and recording who reviewed it in
`reviewer_notes`. `tests/test_localization.py::test_review_status_is_honest`
asserts the current state, so an upgrade has to be deliberate.

## Data

**No patient data was collected.** The real dataset is public, de-identified,
and downloaded rather than vendored. The bundled alternative is synthetic —
generated by `data.generate_synthetic`, statistically shaped to resemble
published summaries of the real file, and containing no record derived from any
real person.

Synthetic-data provenance is propagated into every artifact: the metadata JSON,
every generated Markdown file, the figures (stamped inside the image, since a
figure loses its caption long before it loses its axes), and the app's banner.
`tests/test_pipeline.py::test_synthetic_provenance_propagates_everywhere`
enforces it.

## Before any real-world use

1. Retrain on the real dataset and re-run the calibration study.
2. Validate on Pakistani data. Nothing here establishes that a model trained
   elsewhere in South Asia transfers.
3. Resolve the `BS` sampling condition with whoever collected the data. See
   [UNITS.md](UNITS.md) — the assumption moves the clinical baseline more than
   any modelling choice.
4. Get the Urdu reviewed by a native speaker and a clinician.
5. Establish who is accountable for a missed case, before the tool is in
   anyone's hands. A screening tool without a named owner for its failures is
   not deployable regardless of its metrics.
